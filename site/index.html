NEAR Intents can be audited from outside. Its private chain cannot.

Ask NEAR Intents what it has credited for a token. Ask the token's own contract what NEAR Intents holds. Two calls, any block, no key. We did it for 214 tokens worth $223.8M, and none is short. Run over past blocks, the same audit shows the October exploit in the public books 31 hours 40 minutes before the first payout: ten dollars at first, $1.3M by the end. It stops at one account, the private chain's treasury, which now holds 61.0% of everything, and through which 1Click's swaps now settle. Our rating: Hold, small and staked, to 2029.

01The audit

Two questions, two contracts, one block.

Credited

What NEAR Intents says it owes, to all accounts together.

intents.near.mt_batch_supply(token_ids)

Held

What the token's own contract says NEAR Intents has.

<token>.ft_balance_of("intents.near")

The test

Held at or above credited: whole. Credited above held: a hole, and the difference is its size.

hole = credited - held

Runs in your browser against a public NEAR node: about 25 requests, a few seconds. Nothing is sent anywhere else.

At the pinned block the same audit covers 214 tokens and $223.8M: the listed tokens that have a balance to read, and the unlisted ones the treasury holds. Most are equal to the last unit. The rest hold a little more than is credited. None is short. From a terminal: python3 -m pipeline.audit --now.

Whole on every reading but three.

The 20 largest tokens, audited on every stored reading since February: 4,806 checks, 3 of them short. All three are one token, a bridged dollar token, on 30 September, 1 October and 2 October. On the last of them 98% of everything credited in that token had nothing behind it, and 87% of that credit belonged to the private chain. A stricter test, whether a token's surplus fell below every earlier reading, finds 3 new lows in the same checks: the same three readings and no others.

Does it cry wolf? At 200 random blocks, the test found a hole on 0 of 199 outside October's and a new low on 0 of 199; the one block that fell inside October's hole showed it. That bounds the false-alarm rate per block below about 1.5%, a ceiling and not an estimate.

The one hole was in the books from ten dollars, 31 hours 40 minutes before the money left.

It opens at ten dollars on 29 September, three seconds after the time CertiK gives for the first exploit transaction, and stays under $3,000 for 28 hours. Then it doubles. It passes $1.0M 13 minutes before the first payout, peaks at $3.8M and stays open 76 hours. This is a replay run in November, not live detection. What it adds to the published timeline is that the hole could be seen and sized from outside at every step.

The hole closed with the credited total unchanged, which is consistent with the reported statement that the money was returned. What the chain shows is the balance on NEAR being refilled, not the tokens on BNB Chain.

This is where the audit stops. The largest account on these books is the private chain's treasury. What that chain owes its own users cannot be asked of any contract, and 61.0% of everything NEAR Intents holds is now behind it.

In money: that balance is 6.2 years of the revenue that reaches NEAR's wallets, the cash flow that funds the buyback. October's hole at its largest was 2.1 months of it. These are scales for what the audit cannot see, not estimates of a loss: nothing public says that balance is short.

02The rating

Rating: Hold, small and staked. Up to 1% of a liquid portfolio, to the end of 2029.

The rule is a judgment. Its inputs are read from the chain by this repository's code, except one disclosure, and so is the rating it gives today.

Held staked, because all new supply is paid to stake: 5.6% a year before the validator's fee at the pinned block. A staker's share of the supply changes by +3.0% a year, and the share of a holder who does not stake by -2.4% a year: to the end of 2029, +9.8% against -7.4%.

RatingWhenPosition
AddThe public books are whole, and either coverage is at least 25% in each of the last two 30-day windows, or NEAR has published the private chain's totals per token against a block heightup to 3%
HoldThe public books are whole, and neither the Add nor the Exit condition on coverage holdsup to 1%
ExitA hole in the public books stays open beyond 3 readings, or the audit can no longer be run, or coverage is below 10% in each of the last two 30-day windowsnone

16%

Coverage, read from the chain.

The NEAR reaching NEAR's three revenue wallets over the last 30 days, against the growth of total supply over the same days: 16%, and 20% in the 30 days before. Traced one step back, 74% of it arrived through the private chain's treasury. That is between 10% and 25%, so the rule says hold.

84%

a year, counted in NEAR

What has to be true by 2029.

For buybacks to match new supply by the end of 2029, the revenue that funds them, counted in NEAR, must grow 84% a year, or 59% a year if HSP-029 takes effect. By 2031: 46% a year or 33% a year. No growth rate can be read from the record yet: fitted from different start weeks since February, it runs from -91% to +1,160% a year. The latest 30 days against the 30 before were -17%.

6.2 years

The part nobody outside can check is large.

The balance behind the private chain is 6.2 years of the revenue that funds the buyback: a scale, not a loss, since nothing public says it is short. October's hole at its largest was 2.1 months of it, and it was closed. NEAR's supply is worth 265x that revenue.

The first three are written down with dates in PREREG-AUDIT.md, hashed on 13 November before the next reading existed. The disclosure need not reveal anyone's balance: a proof of liabilities publishes totals and nothing else.

Dated tests, scored by the same code as their readings arrive: the audit's first (G), then the float's (E, F)

First results, from the reading of 14 November, the first taken after the tests were written: the audit's test passed, two of the float's three passed, and one failed. The float had to be at least $136.4M and was $135.2M. One day settles nothing about March; a fail is shown because we said it would be.

04Behind the wall: what the treasury shows

Part of it is paid for.

On 11 June NEAR announced a reward for keeping money on the private side, with the snapshot taken once it held $70M. On 17 September NEAR said that had happened. The float grew $7.9M per 30 days before the programme, $14.3M per 30 days while the first reward was open, and $31.8M per 30 days since. A second round is promised, so nobody outside can separate use from reward-seeking.

    06Check it yourself

    No key, no account, nothing to install. Python and a public node.

    make test

    Unit tests. No network. About a second.

    make check

    Rebuilds every number on this page from the raw readings in memory and compares. No network, writes nothing.

    make verify

    Re-reads a sample of days from NEAR mainnet first, then does the same. Three to fifteen minutes on a public node.

    Each reading is tied to a block height, so it never changes: anyone who reads the recorded heights gets the same bytes. A read that fails is stored as unknown and left out. It is never counted as zero. The checks write nothing. They fail if a dollar amount, a percentage, a multiple, a count, a clock time or a block height on this page or in the thesis is not what the data produces. They do not check dates or numbers written as words.
